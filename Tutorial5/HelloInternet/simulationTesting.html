Simulation and Testing

The core functions of a digital twin are simulation and testing, which allow engineers to test a virtual model before any changes are made to the actual, physical system.

Why Simulation Matters

A digital twin allows developers a safe sandbox to question "what if?" They can do the same stress test on the digital model rather than driving a wind turbine, jet engine, or manufacturing line past its limitations in the real world. Because the twin operates similarly to its physical counterpart, there is no real-world risk and the results are reliable.

Testing Without the Risk

Teams can test thousands of failure scenarios that would be too costly, too risky, or just impossible to perform on the real system because a digital twin can be reset and ran indefinitely. Extreme weather on a turbine, an unexpected power outage in a manufacturing, or years of wear and tear condensed into a single simulation run are examples of this.

From Testing to Confidence

Before an actual modification is made, the purpose of all this virtual testing is to increase confidence rather than just identify issues. Simulation and testing transform the digital twin from a static model into an active tool for improved decision-making, whether it is used to validate a new part, schedule maintenance, or anticipate how a system will age.
